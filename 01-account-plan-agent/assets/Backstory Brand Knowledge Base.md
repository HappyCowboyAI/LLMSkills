## Brand Philosophy & Voice

Backstory is the system of record for modern revenue teams. We gather activity across tools and channels, interpret it in context, and produce a reliable account timeline teams can trust.

### Who We Are

| We Are | We're Not |
|--------|-----------|
| Clear — we make complex data simple | Just Dashboards — we give context, not charts |
| Interpretive — we give data meaning | Abstract or Academic — we speak plainly |
| Engaging — we make revenue compelling | Noisy — we surface signal, not volume |
| Grounded — built on reliable data | Speculative — we report what happened |
| Trustworthy — accurate when it matters | Sales Theater — we back claims with data |
| Action-Oriented — we surface what matters | Hard to Use — clarity is a product requirement |

### Voice Principles

- **Clear** — We say what we mean. No jargon, no filler. If a simpler word works, use it.
- **Confident** — We know our product and trust it. We don't hedge unnecessarily.
- **Engaging** — We make the complex interesting. Data has a story — we help tell it.
- **Direct** — We get to the point. Brevity is a feature.
- **Approachable** — Smart but not academic. Serious but not stiff.

## Brandmarks & Logo Usage

The Backstory brandmark consists of two elements: the **Symbol** (stylized books/files icon) and the **Wordmark**. Together they form the **Logo**.

### Logo Versions

The logo appears in **two versions only**:
- **Black** — on light backgrounds
- **White** — on dark backgrounds

No color versions, gradients, outlines, shadows, or effects are permitted.

### Logo Rules

**Always:** use approved black or white versions only; maintain the exclusion zone (half the logo height); respect minimum sizes (Symbol 32px digital / Full logo 80px digital); use on on-brand background colors only.

**Never:** recolor, tint, or gradient the logo; stretch, skew, distort, or rotate; apply shadows or emboss; place on non-brand colors or busy photography; recreate letterforms; use below minimum sizes.

## Color

Our brand is built first on **black and white**. Clarity, contrast, and restraint define the system. Color is used sparingly and with intent. **Horizon functions as an accent, not a foundation.**

### Color Hierarchy

| Weight | Colors | Usage |
|--------|--------|-------|
| **Neutral (dominant)** | Black, White | Primary visual language — always lead with these |
| **Primary (supporting)** | Graphite, Surface Gray, Horizon | Structure, hierarchy, secondary surfaces |
| **Secondary (accent only)** | Plum, Mint, Cinder, Indigo, Cobalt | Used sparingly; never competing with content |

### Data Visualization

For multi-series charts, use in order: **Horizon → Mint → Plum → Cobalt → La Te light → La Te dark.**

| Token | Light | Dark | Role |
|-------|-------|------|------|
| Data/1 | `#6397AD` | `#7DACC0` | Horizon — primary series |
| Data/2 | `#5BA779` | `#8FCDA8` | Mint — secondary series |
| Data/3 | `#B08FA2` | `#E8DDE3` | Plum — tertiary series |
| Data/4 | `#9FDFFF` | `#21B5FF` | Cobalt — quaternary series |
| Data/5 | `#CEB375` | `#FDFBF7` | La Te — supplementary |
| Data/6 | `#B49650` | `#CEB375` | La Te dark — supplementary |

### Data Visualization Principles

**Do:** use typography and whitespace as the primary organizational tools; apply brand colors only to highlight what matters most; ensure sufficient contrast for accessibility; use Chivo Mono for all numerical labels and data callouts; keep chart backgrounds neutral (White or Graphite) — never colored; use Data/1–6 tokens for chart coloring.

**Don't:** use the full secondary palette as a data palette; apply branding to every data point; use gradients within chart elements; sacrifice readability for brand expression.

## Imagery

**Photography direction:** clean, textural imagery; minimal and intentional; favor natural light and organic textures; avoid generic stock or heavily posed shots.

**Painting elements:** Backstory may incorporate painterly public-domain artwork as a background element, always transformed (reeded glass / subtle refraction) — never reproduced untreated.

**Imagery rules — don't:** use generic stock, apply color filters or heavy effects, introduce off-brand color, reproduce public-domain artwork untreated, place text over imagery without sufficient contrast.
